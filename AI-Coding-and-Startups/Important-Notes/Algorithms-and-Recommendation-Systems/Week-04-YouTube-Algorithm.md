# Week 04 — How YouTube Knows What I Like

## Introduction

YouTube's recommendation system is designed to help users discover videos they are likely to enjoy.

The primary goal is to maximize user satisfaction and engagement.

---

# Core Recommendation Process

The system works in two major stages:

1. Candidate Generation
2. Ranking

---

# Candidate Generation

YouTube first selects a large pool of videos that might interest the user.

Sources include:

- Watch history
- Search history
- Similar users
- Channel subscriptions
- Previously watched videos

---

# Ranking

The selected videos are scored and ranked.

Videos most likely to interest the user move to the top.

---

# User Signals

The algorithm observes:

## Watch Time

How long users watch a video.

---

## Click-Through Rate

How often users click a thumbnail.

---

## Engagement

Includes:

- Likes
- Comments
- Shares

---

## Viewing History

Past viewing behavior influences recommendations.

---

# Personalization

Different users receive different recommendations.

Factors include:

- Interests
- Search behavior
- Watch history
- Device usage
- Region

---

# Why Recommendations Improve

The algorithm continuously learns.

Every interaction becomes feedback.

Examples:

- Watching videos
- Skipping videos
- Liking content
- Subscribing to channels

---

# Benefits

- Faster content discovery
- Personalized experience
- Relevant recommendations

---

# Challenges

- Echo chambers
- Content addiction
- Reduced exposure to diverse viewpoints

---

# Key Learnings

- Recommendations are prediction systems.
- User behavior is the strongest signal.
- Watch history significantly shapes future suggestions.
- Personalization is central to modern content platforms.
- Algorithms aim to predict user interest, not simply popularity.
